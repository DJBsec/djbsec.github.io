## Microsoft Defender Stops QNET Attack in 128 Seconds

<span class="badge badge-primary" style="font-size: 0.7rem;">Ransomware</span>

Microsoft says Defender's automatic attack disruption contained a multistage attack against QNET just 128 seconds after the first high-severity detection. The attacker used the legitimate Windows `mshta.exe` utility to execute malicious content and retrieve a second-stage payload, illustrating a living-off-the-land technique that can evade traditional defenses. Defender correlated multiple signals and automatically isolated the compromised device before the attacker could establish additional persistence, communicate with command-and-control infrastructure, or move laterally. Microsoft says its device-isolation capability acts only after reaching a high-confidence verdict and still allows security teams to review and reverse the action. The incident demonstrates how automated containment can dramatically shrink the window attackers have to turn an initial compromise into a larger ransomware incident.

[Read More](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/08/04/129-seconds-disruption-microsoft-defender-stops-ransomware-qnet/)

## Ransomware Gangs Target Managers With "Business Privilege"

<span class="badge badge-primary" style="font-size: 0.7rem;">Ransomware</span>

Ransomware groups are increasingly targeting managers and other influential employees instead of focusing exclusively on CEOs or technical administrators. Zscaler researchers tracked 351 victims across 334 organizations and found nearly two-thirds held manager-level positions or higher, with an average age of 46. Attackers are pursuing what researchers call "business privilege," targeting people with access to invoices, budgets, contracts, customer accounts, HR records, and the authority to influence ransom decisions. The research also found ransomware attempts blocked across Zscaler's cloud increased 146 percent over the past year, while publicly reported extortion cases climbed 70 percent. The findings suggest organizations should consider business authority and access to sensitive processes when identifying high-risk accounts, not just traditional administrator privileges.

## OpenAI Plans Stronger Security for High-Capability Astra AI

<span class="badge badge-primary" style="font-size: 0.7rem;">Threat Intelligence</span>

OpenAI says it is preparing stronger security controls around its Astra model as increasingly capable AI systems raise concerns about their potential use in sophisticated cyberattacks. Planned protections include isolated testing environments, tighter restrictions on network and tool access, stronger safeguards for model weights, sandboxing, and expanded monitoring. The company also plans to pause certain internal Astra testing when the required protections are unavailable and provide security recommendations to external evaluators. The move comes as AI developers wrestle with models that are becoming increasingly capable of autonomously performing cybersecurity tasks. At the same time, Anthropic is relaxing some restrictions around its Fable models, illustrating different approaches to managing the risks posed by increasingly powerful AI systems.

[Read More](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/08/08/openai-pledges-to-add-astra-security-as-anthropic-loosens-fables-leash/5285161)

## Webmail CSS Attacks Create New Risks for AI-Powered Email

<span class="badge badge-primary" style="font-size: 0.7rem;">Vulnerability</span>

Security research shows that malicious CSS embedded in email can potentially be used to steal credentials, manipulate interfaces, expose sensitive information, and influence AI-powered tools that process inbox content. Researcher Gareth Heyes examined techniques affecting major webmail platforms including Outlook, Gmail, Fastmail, Proton Mail, Yahoo Mail, and AOL Mail. One technique demonstrated how trusted interface elements could be manipulated to resemble password fields, while other attacks explored ways browser and sanitization behavior could expose sensitive information. The issue becomes increasingly important as AI assistants gain permission to read, summarize, and act on email content automatically. The research demonstrates how technologies traditionally considered presentation-focused can create unexpected security boundaries when combined with complex web applications and AI agents.

[Read More](https://securityaffairs.com/196899/hacking/webmail-css-attacks-expose-a-new-risk-for-ai-powered-email-tools.html)

## Phishing Attack Hits U.S. Defense Manufacturer IEH

<span class="badge badge-primary" style="font-size: 0.7rem;">Phishing</span>

U.S. defense and aerospace manufacturer IEH Corporation disclosed that an employee fell victim to a phishing attack that compromised their Microsoft 365 mailbox. The attacker impersonated a prospective business contact and sent a fraudulent Microsoft document-sharing link that directed the employee to a fake login page designed to capture credentials. The compromised mailbox contained customer information, engineering documents, attachments, and potentially export-controlled technical information, although IEH has not confirmed that the data was actually exfiltrated. Investigators also discovered malicious mailbox rules, indicating the attacker attempted to maintain access or intercept communications. The incident demonstrates how a relatively conventional credential-phishing attack can become a serious national-security concern when it compromises an organization within the defense supply chain.

## Microsoft Teams Adds Centralized Security Detection Reporting

<span class="badge badge-primary" style="font-size: 0.7rem;">Threat Intelligence</span>

Microsoft is preparing a new Security Detection Report for the Teams admin center that will give administrators centralized visibility into threats occurring across chats and channels. The dashboard will consolidate detections involving malicious URLs, impersonation attempts, and potentially dangerous file types while providing information about senders, recipients, detection types, and affected conversations. Administrators will also be able to export detection information for SIEM and compliance workflows and block malicious external users directly through Teams settings. Microsoft expects the feature to begin rolling out in late August, with broader availability following in early September. The capability addresses an increasingly important visibility gap as attackers turn collaboration platforms into channels for phishing, impersonation, malicious links, and malware delivery.

[Read More](https://cybersecuritynews.com/teams-security-detection-report/)

## Critical Metabase Zero-Day Exploited to Gain Administrator Access

<span class="badge badge-primary" style="font-size: 0.7rem;">Vulnerability</span>

Metabase has confirmed active exploitation of a critical zero-day vulnerability that can allow unauthenticated attackers to obtain administrator access to vulnerable business intelligence servers. The CVSS 10.0 vulnerability involves an unauthenticated SQL injection affecting a publicly accessible password-reset endpoint in Metabase versions 1.58 and later. Attackers can manipulate database records to gain administrative privileges, potentially exposing sensitive information and credentials for databases connected to the platform. Metabase's cloud environment was targeted on August 3, while other organizations have also reported data exposure associated with exploitation of the vulnerability. Self-hosted customers are being urged to patch immediately, revoke active sessions, audit administrator accounts and API keys, and rotate credentials associated with connected databases.
